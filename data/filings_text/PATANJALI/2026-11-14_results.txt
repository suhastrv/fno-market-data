        Mumbai, 14th November 2026: Patanjali Foods Limited (PFL) today announced its unaudited financial
        results for the Quarter and Half Year ended 30th September 2026.

        Inflationary pressures strengthened during the quarter, with retail inflation rising from approximately
        4.45% in July 2026 to 4.82% in August and 5.54% in September, marking its highest level since the revised
        CPI series began in January 2026.

        The increase was primarily driven by food inflation, which climbed to 7.7%. Price pressures were sharper in
        rural markets, where a deficient monsoon and El Niño conditions weighed on sentiment, even as resilient
        non-farm rural activity continued to cushion household incomes. Overall, the demand environment
        remained supportive during the festive-season indicating healthy consumer momentum.

                                              About Patanjali Foods Limited

           Incorporated in 1986, Patanjali Foods Limited is one of India’s top FMCG players in India. The Company is
           present in Edible Oils, FMCG, and Wind Power Generation segments via a bouquet of brands like Patanjali,
           Ruchi Gold, Nutrela, Dant Kanti, Mahakosh, Sunrich, etc.

           The Company is focused on investing in building brands and expanding its product portfolio and towards
           this, the Company offers food products across multiple categories, price points and segments to cater to a
           wide spectrum of consumer preferences.

           Sustainability remains an integral part of Patanjali Foods' business approach and a major focus, with the
           company continuing to strengthen its sustainability initiatives during FY26, with progress across
           environmental stewardship, responsible sourcing, farmer engagement, and community development. The
           company recorded a ~16% reduction in energy intensity and a ~12% reduction in Scope 1 and 2 emissions
           intensity, along with a 68% reduction in treated water discharge. Renewable fuel consumption increased
           32% YoY, while waste generation declined to 50,775 MT, with no waste incinerated or sent to landfill. Its oil
           palm plantation area under drip irrigation expanded to 1,10,722 hectares, positively impacting 80,579
           farmers through direct FFB procurement. The Company’s wind energy assets generated ~ 62,625 MWh of
           renewable energy, meeting around 27% of its total electricity requirements and contributing to the
           sequestration of 53,254.45 tCO₂ emissions. The company also undertook community development
           initiatives, spending Rs. 41.65 crore on health, education, and sports, including Rs. 26.45 crore on CSR
           activities. On the social front, it maintained 100% health and accident insurance coverage for employees and
           workers, alongside human rights training across the workforce. Board-level oversight through the ESG &
           CSR Committee, along with policies aligned with all nine NGRBC principles, supports its sustainability
