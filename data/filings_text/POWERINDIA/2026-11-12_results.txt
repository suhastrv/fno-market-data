Results for Q2FY27 ended on September 30, 2026:
Hitachi Energy India Limited's order and revenue growth continued in Q2 FY27. The growth has been
supported by strong tailwinds- India’s electricity generation in Q2 FY27 was 536 billion units (BU),
which is up 11% year-on-year, while peak demand met was 270 GW, recorded on 16 July 2026, an
18% increase from the 230 GW peak in Q2 FY26, according to the Central Electricity Authority (CEA).
The country added over 3,000 circuit km of high-voltage transmission lines during July-September
2026, including a record 1,807 ckm in September alone, taking the national transmission network
beyond 512,000 ckm. India has entered an energy intensive phase which indicates strong investments
in modernization of the grid, transmission and digitization of the grid. A steady, systematic shift to
electricity across sectors and industries has further accelerated the pace of energy transition in the
country, with electricity growth directly linked to India’s economic growth.

Commenting on the quarterly results, N Venu, Managing Director & CEO of Hitachi Energy India Ltd.,
said, “At Hitachi Energy India Limited, we anticipate these underlying opportunities and have invested
in new infrastructure and are strengthening our existing capabilities. These efforts reflect in our
Q2FY27 results. Both orders and revenue continue to secure sustained year-on-year (YoY) growth,
backed by disciplined order execution over the quarter.

Orders
Q2FY27 saw a significant 85.9% YoY growth totaling INR 4,120.8 crore, primarily supported by
positive market demand and sustained order momentum. This quarter, transmission orders from
utilities for power transformers, STATCOM, cooling systems, Air-Insulated Switchgear (AIS) &
Gas-Insulated Switchgear (GIS), etc., spearheaded the order book. In terms of segment-wise order

Outlook
Recent market reports suggest that the country’s electricity demand will surpass overall energy
demand within a decade (by 2035), marking an era of electricity. Industrial clusters are emerging as
major centers of energy consumption and grid investment according to the World Economic Forum,
and India's industrialization is increasingly electricity-based rather than fossil-fuel-based.

India’s latest Economic Survey 2024-25 also highlighted infrastructure and industrial expansion as
critical growth enablers. This makes rapid modernization of grids indispensable. Hence, the focus is
on technologies that make the grid more flexible, more visible, and more resilient.

The PM-DHARA scheme too will play a significant role in India’s energy transition journey. Through
the scheme, the policymakers have tried to address key challenges of grid readiness, resilience,
