A little bit of a deep dive into our Advance Battery Materials business. Our battery materials
strategy is progressing from advanced materials to integrated high-value solutions for the
lithium-ion battery industry. Through Nanovace, our 51% joint venture with Australian
technology partner, Kinaltek, we are developing high-purity nano-silicon using a proprietary
chemical reduction process with the potential for lower cost, lower carbon manufacturing.


Silicon, of course, offers 10x the energy storage capacity of graphite, 3,579 mAh/g versus 372
mAh/g, creating opportunities for high energy density and a faster charging. However, managing
silicon's volume expansion of up to 300% remains a critical challenge. That's where our
innovation and program is focused on overcoming these limitations.


Our pilot project in Palej has started creating some samples, making an important milestone in
the scale-up of the technology. Materials produced at the facility are currently undergoing
internal qualification to be followed by customer evaluation and validation. This represents a
significant transition from laboratory scale technology development to pilot scale
manufacturing, strengthening the foundation for future market adoption.


There's another change from last time. We are now moving beyond nano-silicon additives to
silicon carbon composite solutions as well, combining PCBL's expertise in engineered carbons
with Nanovace silicon technology. Our objective is to improve conductivity, manage expansion,
enhance cycle life, and deliver commercially scalable anode materials. Development is
progressing across Palej, Belgium, and Australia, leveraging complementary capabilities in
electrochemistry and carbon engineering.


Just for reference, the global silicon carbon anode market estimated at approximately $1.2 billion

Carbon Black and Acetylene Black programs, this positions PCBL to capture a large share of
