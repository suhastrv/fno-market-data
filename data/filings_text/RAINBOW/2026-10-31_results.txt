Rainbow approach




                      2215

Dr. Ramesh Kancharla
Pediatric Healthcare Visionary
Chairman & Managing Director

Dr. Ramesh comes from an agricultural background from a village in Andhra Pradesh,
India where the accessibility to healthcare was very poor. This motivated Dr. Ramesh to
pursue medical education

Garnered rich experience in pediatric gastroenterology and liver disease at best
pediatric hospitals in the United Kingdom and got exposed to the nuances of pediatric
healthcare model at these hospitals

Lack of quality pediatric healthcare in the country and his resolve that no child should
suffer due to lack of dedicated medical facilities led to the foundation of Rainbow
Children’s Hospitals in 1999

Rainbow is currently the country’s largest pediatric hospital chain with 26 hospitals
spread across 11 cities


Bestowed with Healthcare leader of year award by the Financial Express, Healthcare
award, 2024

Annexure: Abbreviations and Definitions


 Definitions:
 • Capacity Beds : Total Bed count in the hospital on the last day of the relevant period /year. (Capacity beds are a total of operational beds (bed available for mid-night
   occupancy) and non-census beds (all other bed available other than census beds, i.e., day-care beds, pre-OP & post-OP beds, Emergency Beds, etc).
 • Operational Beds: Weighted average count of census beds for the relevant period /year. (Beds where patient stays for treatment and counted for midnight occupancy)
 • Occupied beds days: Total count of patients at midnight at each day for the relevant period / year
 • Occupied beds: Occupied bed days divided by no. of the days for the relevant period/year
 • Occupancy : Occupied beds/Operational Beds for the relevant period / year
 • ARPOB : Revenue divided by occupied bed days
 • ALOS : Occupied bed days divided by IP Discharges
 • IP Discharges: Count of patient discharged for the relevant period / year
 • OP Consultation: Count of doctor consultation in outpatient department for the relevant period / year

 Abbreviations
 • INR: Indian Rupee
 • Mn: Million
 • ARPOB: Average Revenue Per Occupied Bed Day
 • ALOS: Average Length of Stay
 • Revenue: Revenue from Operations + Other Income as per financials
 • EBITDA: Profit before tax + finance costs + depreciation and amortization – other income
 • EBITDA Margin: EBITDA divided by Revenue in percentage
 • PAT: Profit After Tax
 • PAT Margin: PAT divided by Revenue in percentage
 • NABH : National Accreditation Board for Hospitals & Healthcare Providers
 • NABL : National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories
 • JCI : Joint Commission International
 • DNB: Diplomate of National Board
 • DrNB: Doctorate of National Board
 • MRCPCH: Membership of the Royal College of Pediatrics and Child Health

South India’s youngest baby successfully receives liver transplant




 •   A six-week-old baby weighing just 2.9 kg developed jaundice in the first week of
     life, progressing rapidly to severe liver failure by the third week. Investigations
     revealed a rare condition called Gestational Alloimmune Liver Disease (GALD),
     and initial treatments with intravenous immunoglobulin and two exchange
     transfusions failed to halt the deterioration.
 •   With the baby’s life at risk, Rainbow Children’s Hospital, Banjara Hills,
     performed a complex living-donor liver transplant using a 62-gram graft from the
     mother. The procedure was particularly challenging due to the baby’s tiny blood
     vessels and limited abdominal space, requiring exceptional surgical precision to
     connect the blood vessels and position the graft safely.
 •   The post-operative journey was quite stormy with pulmonary complication, and
     the child required an additional intestinal surgery for obstruction. The baby was
     managed on parenteral nutrition during the ICU stay of 3 weeks and discharged
     after another week of stay with a well functioning liver.
 •   This landmark surgery demonstrates Rainbow’s ability to perform highly
     complex neonatal transplants, combining advanced surgical expertise,
     specialised intensive care and multidisciplinary teamwork to save even the
     smallest and most critically ill patients.

Uncovering a Rare Kidney Disorder in a 2.5-Year-Old Child




   •   A previously healthy 2.5-year-old child presented with widespread body swelling and significant protein loss through
       the urine, indicating a serious kidney disorder. Despite six weeks of standard steroid treatment for nephrotic
       syndrome, the condition did not improve, raising concerns about steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome.
   •   The kidney biopsy revealed an extremely rare condition called lipoprotein glomerulopathy, in which abnormal fat
       deposits accumulate within the tiny blood vessels of the kidneys. Genetic testing confirmed an associated variation
       in the APOE gene, helping establish the diagnosis. This was reported as the third and youngest Pediatric case of
       its kind in India.
   •   Following the diagnosis, steroids were gradually withdrawn and lipid-lowering treatment with atorvastatin was
       initiated. Within just three weeks, urinary protein loss reduced significantly, while the child's lipid profile and blood
       albumin levels improved.
   •   This case highlights the need of Pediatric nephrology services with advanced genetic testing in identifying and
       treating rare kidney diseases, enabling targeted treatment and potentially improving long-term outcomes.

Born with an Extra Limb: A Remarkable Surgical Journey


 •   A full-term newborn, born to a mother with gestational diabetes, was noted at birth to
     have a rare and striking abnormality—an extra limb-like structure arising from the
     perineal region, a condition known as polymelia (pyromelia).
 •   Despite this, the baby cried at birth and was initially stable.
 •   As such malformation can be associated with serious internal defects, a detailed
     evaluation was urgently undertaken. Brain and abdominal ultrasounds were normal.
     Imaging of the spine showed a low-lying spinal cord with an associated fatty mass,
     suggesting a condition called lipomyelocele, a form of closed spinal dysraphism.
 •   Advanced scans also confirmed that the extra limb was not connected to the spinal
     cord, consistent with a rare condition called polymelia (extra limb formation).
 •   A multidisciplinary team involving neonatology, pediatric surgery, radiology,
     anesthesia, and neurosurgery performed the complex surgery.
 •   The baby underwent successful surgical excision of the perineal limb appendage. The
     operation was delicate due to the proximity to the spine, pelvis, and rectum, but the
     abnormal structures were completely removed without neurological injury.
 •   The parents were counselled for follow up for the associated spinal dysraphism.
 •   This rare case highlights how a life born with an extraordinary anatomical anomaly
     can be safely guided toward a normal future through precise imaging,
     multidisciplinary care, and timely surgery.
