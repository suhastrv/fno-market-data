The Indian Space Research
Organisation(Isro)isallsetto
launch its next navigation
satellite, NVS-03, aboard the
GSLV-F1, the rocket’s 20th
mission — in a crucial move
to rebuild India’s ailing
regional satellite system,
Navigation with Indian Con-
stellation (NavIC) — today
from the Second Launch Pad
at the Satish Dhawan Space
Centre      in    Sriharikota,
Andhra Pradesh.
    NavIC currently has only
three satellites available for
positioning, navigation and
timing services, below the
minimum requirement of
four. Though the second-
generationNVSseriesstarted
beingplacedinorbit,starting
with NVS-01 in 2023, the fail-
ure of NVS-02 in January
2025     aboard      GSLV-F15
delayed the launch of NVS-
03, NVS-04 and NVS-05.
    NavIC aims to provide
position, navigation and
timing services across India
and around 1,500 km beyond
the country’s landmass.
    Key highlights of the cur-
rent mission include an
indigenous atomic clock and
support for the L1 navigation
frequency alongside L5 and
S-band signals.
    “The launch of NVS-03 is
a signiﬁcant milestone for
India’s indigenous naviga-
tion capabilities, particularly
given the gaps in the NavIC
constellation over the past
few years,” said Lt Gen AK
Bhatt(Retd),directorgeneral,
Indian Space Association.
    “NavICplaysacriticalrole
in defence operations, mari-
time security and civilian
applications while reducing
dependence on foreign navi-
gation systems,” he said,
adding that strengthening
the constellation is essential
at a time when strategic
autonomy and national
security are paramount.
