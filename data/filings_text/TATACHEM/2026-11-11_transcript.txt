               highlights across our markets and geographies during the quarter and first half of the year.

               geographies. In the Living Essentials, which is mainly bicarbonate, salt, and FOS, the demand
               outlook remains supported by premiumization, increasing consumption acrossfood, feed,
               pharma, and beverage applications. Salt and bicarbonate are expected to continue to provide
               stable growth, while prebiotics offer attractive long-term opportunities driven by increasing
               customer focus on gut health and wellness.


               In terms of Industrial Essentials, which is mainly soda ash and silica, the near- to medium-term
               outlookfor the global soda ash industry continues to remain challenging. Market conditions are
               characterized by persistent oversupply, subdued industrial demand, especially in China, and
               intense competition across export markets. Significant capacity additions in China over the last
               two years, coupled with slower-than-expected demand recovery in end-use sectors, have
               continued to pressure global soda ash prices and producer margins. The ongoing conflict in the
               Middle East, including disruptions impacting regional shipping routes and energy markets, has
               increased freight volatility and supply chain risks. While large-scale demand destruction has not
               materialized, in fact, in some parts of the world, the demand is robust, customers remain cautious
               in procurement decisions amid macroeconomic uncertainty. China remains the key determinant
               of global market dynamics. High operating rates, continued exports and competitive pricingfrom
               Chinese producers continue to influence global soda ash prices and trade flows. This is coupled
               with weak domestic demand from China's real estate and construction sectors, resulting in
               additional export volumes entering the international market.


               Amidst all this, India continues to outperform most global markets. Demand growth remains
               healthy, supported by strong activity right across segments. Capacity expansion by solar glass
               manufacturers and continued infrastructure investments are expected to continue to provide
               above-average global demand growth. Southeast Asia remains highly competitive. While the

In terms of Farm Essentials, uneven rainfall and delayed sowing affected demand during parts
of the quarter. Although Kharif acreage subsequently recovered, the broader agriculture outlook
remains cautious, supported by crop coverage, improving irrigation, policy support, technology
adoption, and resilient rural demand. However, weather volatility, crop price movements, and

In terms of supply scenario, broadly, mainland China continues to be the driver of supplies,
including in bicarbonate, which saw increased supply on account of new capacity. The global
soda ash market, as has already been highlighted, is beset with high Chinese capacity growth
and inventory levels. Export volumes from China remain elevated. By the end of September,
producer inventory had reached nearly 1.89 million metric tons. Inner Mongolia Berun's phase
one, 5 million tons, underwent maintenance outages in October.This capacity is expected to
return to full operating ratesat the end of the month. Meanwhile, Qinghai Salt Lake, a Solvay-
based soda ash producer, will reduce operating rates at the end of October. The magnitude and
duration of these impacts remain uncertain. In addition, Jinjing Technology, parent company of
Shandong Haitian, also announced that they willclose its soda ash and bicarbonate production
lines. Recent announcement by a U.S. producer to reduce production at Wyoming by 1.5 million
tons and exit uneconomic export volume underscores the severity of current market conditions.
This remains one of the key concerns, that is, persistent low export realization and the structural
demand-supply imbalance which are facing the global soda ash industry.


Within India, DGTR has recommended quantity restrictions on soda ash imports for two years
after concluding that imports have surged and are causing serious injury to domestic industry.
Imports increased by 89% in FY2024 and have remained at elevated levels, driven by global
oversupply and capacity additions in export countries and diversion of export volumes to India.


The pricing environment remains under pressure for soda ash. The Indian domestic pricing
softened towards the later part of the year following improved import availability and increased
domestic supply. U.S. export pricing remains under pressure. Looking ahead, pricing is expected
to remain range bound as market participantscontinue to contend with elevated inventory level.
Any sustained improvement in prices is likely todepend on a combination of supply
rationalizationand strong demand growth in the applications and in the geographies.
