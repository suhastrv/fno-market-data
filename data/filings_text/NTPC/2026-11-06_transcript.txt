Rising demand during both peak and non-peak hours creates growth opportunities. And
reinforces the need for reliable baseload power supply. NTPC's generation portfolio and NGEL's
renewable business complement each other in meeting this trend of demand. Accordingly, we
are combining the strength of both organizations and developing new business models to meet
the country's growing power needs and create long-term value for our shareholders through
disciplined investment into value-accretive projects.


At the same time, the grid is becoming more complex. Rapid renewable penetration is increasing
backing-down requirements during solar hours and creating steep evening ramps. To address
this, we are already implementing 5 gigawatt hour of battery energy storage system at our
thermal power stations alongside pursuing other long-duration storage opportunities.


NTPC Group delivered a strong operational performance during the second quarter. The growth
in generation, captive coal dispatch and power trading volumes indicate robust growth in every
business.


However, due to transmission constraint, NGEL is facing curtailment of power from RE
projects, which is a phenomenon faced by other RE projects in the country too. To address this
issue, NGEL has plans to implement 40 gigawatt-hours of battery for which proposals are under
process.


In addition to the above, NGEL has entered into contracts for 3 gigawatt-hours of storage in
service mode, and additional 12 gigawatt-hours are under tendering in service mode.


To address transmission constraints and renewable energy curtailment, the Government of India
approved the Green Energy Corridor Phase 3 schemes on 30th September 2026. The scheme
will strengthen intrastate transmission systems to evacuate up to 135 gigawatts of renewable
power and provide 50 gigawatt-hour of battery energy storage. It has a total project outlay of
INR1,86,405 crores with central financial support of INR54,082 crores and is targeted for
completion by FY32-33.


Coal generation continued to provide reliable power and help balance the grid, particularly as
hydro output was lower during the quarter. According to the CEA report, the plant load factor
of coal plants across the country rose to 70.37% in H1 FY27 compared to 66.02% in H1 last
year. Higher PLF has increased coal consumption, which continued to exceed supply.


Accordingly, several coal stations in the country have critical coal stock levels. Presently, we
have 5.2 MMT of coal stock at our stations, sufficient for 7 days of coal requirement. Despite
supply constraint from CIL and Singareni Collieries Company Limited. dispatches from our
captive mines grew by nearly 21% in H1 FY27.


This was made possible by better monsoon preparedness at our coal mines and effective planning
of our coal loading infrastructure. We have also issued tenders to procure 13 MMT of coal for
H2 FY27 from alternative sources including commercial mines, Orissa Power Generation
Company, NLC and West Bengal.
