The first half of this financial year was characterised by significant external volatility. The conflict in West Asia continued to
disrupt global trade flows, with vessels rerouted around the Cape of Good Hope, extending transit times by 10 – 14 days,

rates remained elevated and European gas prices were more than twice the January 2026 levels. Meanwhile, Chinese steel
exports remained persistently high at 86 million tons year-to-date and are expected to again exceed 100 million tons this
calendar year.

Against this backdrop, our focus at Tata Steel has been the same: strengthening earnings, maintaining operational agility,
and improving cost efficiency to improve cash generation. Our performance in the first half of this financial year is best
assessed through this lens. Consolidated revenues stood at Rs 1,26,231 crores and EBITDA was Rs 20,035 crores, which
translates to an EBITDA margin of about 16%. Consolidated EBITDA improved by 21% or Rs 2,160 per ton on YoY basis.
These results reflect the strength of our India business, supported by disciplined cost management and capital allocation.

I would now like to elaborate on three themes. Firstly, the quality of our India earnings and plans to grow both volume and
product portfolio. Secondly, our assessment of the evolving situation in the Netherlands and the UK; and finally, our focus on
cash generation and continued cost transformation, with the launch of a multi-year program to drive efficiencies, operating
model resets, and cost takeouts to sustain competitiveness.

Let me start with India, which continues to be the anchor of our performance and accounts for nearly three-fourths of our
crude steel production. In a quarter that is traditionally impacted by monsoons and weaker construction activity, as Naren
mentioned, we delivered our strongest-ever second quarter performance. Healthy demand enabled growth of 16% QoQ in
deliveries and outpaced the highest-ever second-quarter production.

At the same time, raw material and logistics costs increased meaningfully during the quarter. Total raw material costs were
higher by about Rs 2,408 crores. While benchmark coking coal prices increased by 12% between July and September, the
impact on our imported coal consumption cost was limited to around 3%, or approximately Rs 250 crores, reflecting the
benefits of our coal blending strategy and increased focus on leaner coals. The balance of the increase was primarily driven
by higher inbound logistics costs, reflecting elevated freight rates, demurrage and vessel charter expenses, as well as higher
purchases of coke and scrap. Inventory drawdown of around 68 kt in the second quarter compared with a build-up in the first

QoQ basis. However, this was partly offset by improvement in conversion costs to the extent of Rs 3,317 per ton, driven by
operating leverage on account of higher volumes and cost take-outs. As a result of the above, Standalone EBITDA per ton
moderated to around Rs 17,257 per ton while absolute EBITDA increased 10% QoQ to Rs 10,315 crores.

NINL recorded an EBITDA of Rs 318 crores at a margin of 21%. The 4.8 MTPA expansion project has entered the execution
phase and remains on track for commissioning in FY2030. Site development works, including road and area grading, are
underway, while ordering of key equipment such as the blast furnace package, continues to advance. The project comprises
four long-product rolling mills, including rebar and wire rod facilities. Although the overall ramp-up will be aligned with the
commissioning of the steelmaking complex, one rebar mill is expected to come on stream earlier, enabling an initial production
ramp-up ahead of the upstream facilities. The amalgamation of NINL with Tata Steel remains on track for completion this
financial year, subject to final NCLT approval which is scheduled for hearing later in November.

Volume growth remains important for us, but equally so is increasing the value intensity of our portfolio. Our downstream
projects are progressing well. The 0.7 MTPA HRPGL project has entered the construction phase, with structural erection and
piping works underway. This first-of-its-kind facility in India will serve the energy, automotive and construction segments and
introduce galvannealed products, enabling Tata Steel to address customer requirements and support import substitution.
The doubling of tinplate capacity is also advancing with civil and structural works underway and will aid in market consolidation
in the packaging segment. Alongside these, we recently launched Colorcoat, our globally recognised pre-painted steel brand,

tubes business and ready-to-use construction solutions to address evolving customer needs. Our downstream growth will
continue to get priority as we move forward.

Let me now turn to Netherlands. The key development during the quarter was the restart of the Direct Sheet Plant. In the first
quarter, we effectively lost almost the entire output of this facility, which represents around 1.4 million tons of annual
processing capacity and about 18% of the total volumes in the Netherlands. The plant successfully restarted in early August
under the terms of agreement with the regulator and is presently operating at rated capacity, subject to periodic review.
Importantly, the technical measures implemented are delivering the intended outcomes, with Chromium-6 emissions reducing
materially and currently remaining below prescribed limits. The significance of the restart extends well beyond volumes. It
strengthens our product mix, improves the efficiency of the integrated production chain and lowers conversion costs. As a
result, EBITDA increased from €4 million in the first quarter to €72 million in the second quarter, which translates to about
€56 per ton. Turning to the broader situation in the Netherlands. Since the signing of the Joint Letter of Intent in September
2025, we have completed engineering and technical work on the integrated health and decarbonisation project. While this
work has further matured the DRI – EAF pathway, it has also reinforced our view that the long-term competitiveness of the
IJmuiden site will be determined by the resolution of a number of critical external factors, rather than project design alone.

Firstly, discussions continue with the Regulator and the Province of North Holland regarding the timing and sequencing of
the proposed closure of Coke & Gas Plants 2 and 1. Our position remains unchanged: any closure process must be executed

to engage constructively with all stakeholders to support an orderly transition. However, the timing and outcome of the
process remain uncertain. Secondly, we remain engaged on the evolving framework governing steel slag management.
Temporary disruptions to slag off-take have resulted in additional stockpiling at site. A range of mitigation measures are being
pursued, including technical assessments, alternative storage and disposal solutions, and ongoing stakeholder engagement.
More broadly, the economics of both the existing business and any future decarbonisation pathway will depend on the
resolution of these matters, together with greater visibility on long-term energy affordability, capital requirements, permitting

Moving to the UK, the market has become very challenging. While the second-quarter net realisations improved by around
£72/t on a QoQ basis, deliveries declined to about 0.4 million tons. The key point is that the effective market available to UK
producers has essentially become smaller. Domestic demand remains weak, with automotive and construction activity
expected to decline by 2 – 4% in the current calendar year, while import pressure remains elevated. Between April and July
2026, imports averaged around 738 kt per month, about 22% above the 2025 levels. This has resulted in flat steel inventories
in the UK rising to roughly 865 kt by September, nearly 60% above normal levels and equivalent to almost three months of
demand. With the change in quotas, the market is rebalancing, but it is taking longer than what we had initially estimated to
liquidate old inventories. Having said that, it is important to view the UK business through the lens of the broader Tata Steel
system rather than as a standalone operation. While TSUK continues to report EBITDA losses during this transition period,
its downstream assets provide strategic access to customers and end markets and, in many respects, operate as an
extension of Tata Steel India's value chain. As India's steel production continues to grow, these assets effectively represent
a fifth hot strip mill for the Tata Steel Group, enabling Tata Steel to convert substrate into higher-value products closer to
customers. Consequently, while Tata Steel UK is currently loss-making on a standalone basis, the wider system economics
and value creation for Tata Steel remain materially more positive than what is reflected in the UK entity's standalone
performance. Further, the exports from the UK into the EU are now subject to quota restrictions, creating an additional
constraint on market access. We continue to engage closely with the Government of UK and trade authorities on measures
that improve market balancing and ensure the trade framework better reflects prevailing market conditions.

Turning to the transition project, execution remains on track. The Llanwern pickle line has fully ramped up, and the 3.2 MTPA
EAF project continues to progress in line with plan. Major enabling works have been completed and key contracts have been
awarded. As project activity ramps up, our engagement with the UK Government and other stakeholders is increasingly
focused on ensuring an appropriate investment framework, trade quotas, competitive energy prices and timely grid
connectivity, all of which are important to the long-term competitiveness of the site.

Overall, operating cashflow before capex was Rs 5,160 crores for the quarter. Capital expenditure was Rs 3,280 crores in
the quarter, taking the first half spend to Rs 6,860 crores. During the quarter, the Company also paid a dividend of around
Rs 5,000 crores. Consistent with our capital allocation framework, cashflows continue to be prioritised towards growth
opportunities in India. This is reflected in the strong returns being generated by the business, with Return on Invested Capital
