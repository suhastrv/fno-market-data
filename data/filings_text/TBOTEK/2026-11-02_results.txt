Founders’ Message
Dear Fellow Shareholders,

This quarter was about continuity, execution and delivery. While the macro environment continued to be challenging
in many ways, the business continued to deliver – Monthly transacting buyers reached an all-time high as we continue
to reap the benefits of last year’s investments, GTV for the organic business was up 22% YoY, Adj. EBITDA growth
continued to outpace Gross profit growth driven the platform’s inherent operating leverage, the integration efforts at
Classic Vacations remains on track, we continue to build the platform capabilities in line with our thesis on luxury
complex trips.

Our investments in strengthening our commercial presence and expanding agent relationships continue to deliver
results. Our expanded KAM base enables us to engage with more agents. As new agents become more familiar with
our self-service platform, they require less support, freeing up capacity for our commercial teams to develop new
relationships. This ability to support a growing business without a proportionate increase in costs is fundamental to
TBO’s platform economics and the inherent operating leverage.

Our conviction in the opportunity ahead remains strong. The breadth of our global network gives us access to diverse
travel markets, while our relationships with suppliers provide a foundation for deeper participation in those markets.
Our strategic priorities centre on expanding this distribution, strengthening differentiated supply and consistently
improving the value we deliver to our partners.

Luxury and complex travel are particularly compelling opportunities within this strategy. Personal advice, trusted
relationships and reliable service play an important role in these journeys. By combining our technology and global
reach with richer content, curated supply and broader trip capabilities, we aim to help advisors serve more of their
customers’ travel needs through TBO.

Classic Vacations, which has now completed its first year with TBO, adds meaningful depth to this proposition. Its
established advisor relationships, luxury expertise and supplier partnerships complement our wider platform. Our
focus remains on integrating these strengths thoughtfully, supporting its commercial capabilities and preserving the
service standards associated with the Classic Vacations brand. During the quarter, we meaningfully expanded the sales
team at Classic to allow it to maintain a high touch relationship with key customers while actively expanding the
customer base. These costs will be partially offset by cost optimization initiatives. The impact of such initiatives are
already showing early green shoots in the form of a small yet directionally pertinent margin growth in the Classic
vacations P&L on a trailing 12 month basis (2.5% Adj. EBITDA conversion from GTV vs 2.4% estimated at the time of
the acquisition).

Technology remains central to how we strengthen our proposition and improve productivity. Our focus on AI, simpler
workflows and greater operating efficiency supports both better service and a lower cost to serve. Alongside
disciplined investment in distribution and supply, these capabilities are important to building a more competitive and
scalable business.

We remain focused on creating enduring shareholder value through profitable growth, thoughtful capital allocation
while continuously striving to perfect the underlying consistent execution capabilities.

In the section below, we address the key questions that we think investors might have

Q1 With no new investments in expanding the KAM base, has the Monthly Active Agents growth flattened?




Note: * Does not include Jumbo Online and Classic Vacations


No, the Monthly Active Agents number has continued to grow with broad based strength across relevant markets. As
discussed earlier, a KAM continued to add agents on the platform for several quarters and their saturation thresholds
are fairly high, as agents require less and less time from the KAMs after their first 5 to 10 transaction. This is
fundamental to the platform nature of our business and a key driver of the operating leverage clearly visible in our
reported financials in the current and the previous quarter. While we continue to see several whitespaces in the
market and expanding the KAM base to address these whitespaces will always be an optionality, but the current KAM
base will also continue to drive growth for several quarters before reaching close to saturation.

Q2 How was the performance of Classic Vacations over the Quarter?
This was a key quarter for Classic Vacations for several reasons. On 30th September, Classic Vacations completed a full
year with TBO Tek with their financials now being completely baked into the TBO base. Q2 is also the weakest quarter
for the North America market in terms of seasonality, which is reflected in the GTV for Classic Vacations. This soft
seasonality coupled with the partially fixed SG&A base also drove reverse operating leverage for the quarter,
contrasting with the positive operating leverage demonstrated in Q1 which is the peak of seasonality for the North
America market (for check-ins). Adj. EBITDA as a % of GTV for Q2’FY27 was 1.3% compared to 3.4% in Q1’FY27 and
2.5% on a trailing 12 month basis. This 2.5% GTV to Adj. EBITDA conversion is slightly above our initial base case of
2.4%, as reported at the time of the acquisition.

Q3 Gross Profit for the organic Hotels + Ancillary business (without Classic Vacations) as percentage of GTV
contracted to 5.2% for the previous quarter, is there a recovery in the current quarter?

The contraction is gross profit margins in the organic Hotels + Ancillary business (without Classic Vacations) in Q1’FY27
was driven by 2 distinct yet related factors, the saliency of the Middle East business in the overall GTV mix and a
calibrated call by management to prioritize volume preservation in the Middle East market where the contraction in
the overall market demand led to heightened competitive intensity in the market.

Over the quarter (Q2’FY27) we saw increasing normalization of the margins, and we reported 5.5% gross profit as a
percentage of the organic Hotels and ancillary GTV. This metric stood at 5.6% in Q2’FY26, so while we may not have
achieved full normalization, the metric is moving in a positive direction.

It is also worth noting that while our Middle East market may have reported a 10% YoY growth in GTV on a constant
currency basis, the underlying market hasn’t fully recovered and the competitive intensity in the market remains
elevated with the situation in the region remaining fluid.

Q4 The rate of growth of the SG&A expenses of the organic business was higher than Q1, can you delve deeper
into individual cost heads within the SG&A bucket?

Before we discuss individual cost buckets, it is worth noting that a meaningful chunk of the various cost heads sits in
international markets, and hence the reported cost would be impacted by foreign exchange fluctuations. On a constant
currency basis, the YoY growth in SG&A expenses was 8.6%.

SG&A expenses for the organic business also tapered higher during the quarter partially due to the annual merit
increase in payroll becoming effective starting Q2, reflected in the 12.5% YoY increase in Employee benefit expenses
and Business support services, cumulatively.

As mentioned in the previous quarters, our successful interventions in optimizing hosting and bandwidth costs resulted
in a minimal 2.3% YoY increase despite the business volumes growing meaningfully faster.

Q5 You have reported an exceptional expense of ₹8.1Cr, can you explain the nature of this expense?
Subsequent to quarter end, a wholly owned overseas subsidiary (the subsidiary) of the Holding Company identified
certain irregularities involving suspected collusion among certain current and former employees of the subsidiary and
the Holding Company. The subsidiary has appointed an external expert to conduct a comprehensive investigation and
assessment, and an independent law firm for legal support on the matter. The subsidiary and the Holding Company
have initiated appropriate legal actions with the relevant authorities. Basis the preliminary assessment, the
management of the subsidiary and the Holding Company has estimated a net financial loss of ₹8.1Cr ($ 0.85 million)
in the subsidiary. The final loss may differ on completion of the detailed assessment.

Considering the non-recurring nature of the incident, the loss has been presented as an exceptional item in the
financial results for the quarter and period ended September 30, 2026. The Group has an Insurance for such
matters/incidents and has initiated the process for the insurance claim in respect of above matter.

Q6 On 21st October, the company filed an intimation with stock exchanges regarding an Enforcement Directorate
adjudication order, can you please explain the matter and the outcome in detail?

In relation to FEMA matter disclosed earlier by the Company, we have received an order dated October 16, 2026,
passed by the Special Director (Adjudication), Directorate of Enforcement on October 21, 2026, wherein penalty
amounting to ₹ 222.16 Mn on the Company and ₹24.68 Mn on each Joint Managing Director has been imposed.

The Company, in consultation with its legal counsel, is in the process of filing an appeal against the Adjudication Order.
Based on the legal advice received, the Company is of the view that the prospects of success in the appeal are high
and that the penalties imposed pursuant to the Adjudication Order are unlikely to be sustained. Accordingly, pending
the outcome of the appeal, no adjustments have been made to the financial results for the period ended September
30, 2026.

Q7 Can you quantify the P&L impact of any foreign exchange fluctuations over the quarter?
The total foreign exchange loss for the quarter was ₹8.9Cr, comprising FX conversion costs, hedging premiums,
revaluation movements and the impact of intercompany loans. The current quarter also includes FX and hedging costs
for Classic Vacations, which were not included in Q2’FY26. This compares with a net foreign exchange gain of ₹0.45Cr
in Q2’FY26, where higher revaluation gains offset the FX conversion and hedging costs.

Definitions of key terms used in relation to business

    1. Monthly Transacting Buyers are the average number of Buyers with net positive sales (which is calculated as
        fresh bookings minus cancellations) during each month computed for the relevant year / period from Buyers
        in a particular source market.
    2. GTV - Source Market is computed as total transaction value net of cancellations during the year / period
        generated from a particular source market.
    3. GTV Mix % - Source Market is computed as GTV of a particular source market divided by total GTV for the
        relevant year / period.
    4. GTV – Product is computed as total transaction value net of cancellations during the year / period generated
        from sale of airline tickets and hotel and ancillary bookings on all our platforms.
    5. GTV Mix % - Product is computed as a particular product GTV divided by total GTV for the relevant year /
        period.
    6. Revenue from Operations - Product means revenue recognized on (a) sale of airline tickets (b) Hotel and
        Ancillary bookings and (c) other miscellaneous products like TBO Academy and white label services, on all our
        platforms.
    7. Take Rate % - Product is computed as revenue from operations from particular product divided by such
        product’s GTV for the relevant year / period.
    8. Gross Profit - Product is computed as revenue from operations from the product less service fee for the
        relevant year / period.
    9. Revenue from Operations - Source Market means revenue recognized on sale of airline, hotel and ancillary
        bookings created by buyers in the relevant source market.
    10. Take Rate % - Source Market is computed as revenue from operations from a particular source market
        divided by GTV from such source market for the relevant year.
    11. Gross Profit - Source Market is computed as revenue from operations from a particular source market less
        service fee for the relevant year / period.
    12. EBITDA is calculated as profit/(loss) before tax plus finance costs plus depreciation and amortization
        expenses plus net loss on foreign exchange differences plus exceptional items minus other income.
    13. Adjusted EBITDA is calculated as EBITDA plus share issue expenses plus employee stock option expense plus
        share of loss of joint ventures
    14. EBITDA Margin % is calculated as a percentage of EBITDA divided by revenue from operations.
    15. Adjusted EBITDA Margin % is calculated as a percentage of Adjusted EBITDA divided by revenue from
        operations.
