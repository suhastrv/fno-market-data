Mumbai, India: JSW Steel Limited ("JSW Steel" or the "Company") today reported its financial results for
the Second Quarter ended 30 th September 2026 ("Q2 FY27" or the "Quarter").

    ■    Highest ever Crude Steel Production: 7 .27 million tonnes
    ■    Highest ever Saleable Steel Sales: 7.46 million tonnes
    ■    Revenue from Operations: ,54,486 crores

Global economic activity remained resilient during the quarter despite continued geopolitical
uncertainties and resulting inflation. The IMF recently upgraded its global growth forecast for 2026 by
10 basis points to 3.1%, while maintaining its 2027 outlook at 3.4%. Al-led investments, improving
manufacturing indicators and resilient consumer demand have provided support to growth. However,
ongoing conflict in the Middle East continues to create uncertainty through its impact on energy
markets and commodity prices, while inflation and fiscal concerns have kept bond yields elevated and
prompted major central banks to hike rates.

Among major economies, the US continues to demonstrate resilience, aided by ongoing investments in
technology and artificial intelligence, and strong consumer spending. In China, robust exports have
supported manufacturing activity, while recent policy measures aimed at the real estate sector and
infrastructure development are expected to provide support to domestic growth amidst weak demand.

India remains a bright spot in the global economy, with growth being driven by resilient domestic
demand, a robust investment cycle and improving industrial activity. Reflecting confidence in the
economy's underlying strength, the RBI has raised its FY27 GDP growth forecast to 7.1% from 6.7%
earlier. Industrial production and exports have remained healthy despite external headwinds, with
engineering exports continuing to demonstrate resilience. Momentum in the automotive sector also
remains strong, supported by healthy consumer demand and sustained economic activity.

India's steel consumption remained strong, growing 7.0% YoY to 43.4mt in Q2 FY27 and by 7.5% in Hl
FY27, supported by steady domestic demand as well as healthy exports of engineering goods. During
Hl, exports and imports of total steel (including semi-finished steel) were broadly balanced. However,
India remained a net importer of finished steel. In China, steel production and consumption continued
to decline. During January-September CY26, steel production fell by 2.9% while consumption contracted
by 3.9% . While finished steel exports declined 2% during this period, total steel exports including semi-
finished steel, increased 3.3%, driven by a 46% YoY rise in semi-finished steel exports.

Outlook

The IMF has upgraded its 2026 global growth forecast to 3.1% from 3.0% projected earlier and maintained
the 2027 forecast at 3.4%, reflecting continued resilience in the global economy despite geopolitical
uncertainties and inflationary pressures. Global growth continues to be supported by strong Al-driven
investments and India's relative outperformance . However, ongoing tensions in the Middle East continue
to create supply-side risks and keep energy and commodity prices elevated. Inflationary and fiscal
concerns have contributed to higher bond yields, prompting major central banks to maintain a cautious
policy stance. Looking ahead, easing geopolitical tensions could lead to reconstruction-led demand and
boost growth.
In the United States, economic growth continues to be supported by robust Al-related investments and
resilient consumer spending despite inflationary pressures .
China's manufactu ring sector continues to expand, supported by strong export momentum. Recent
measures aimed at supporting the real estate sector and infrastructure investment should support
economic activity going forward, although weak domestic demand continues to remain a concern .
In the Eurozone, improving PMI trends and a strong GDP print for Q2CY26 reflect economic resilience,
even as higher energy costs continue to weigh on the growth outlook.
India continues to remain the fastest-growing major economy, underpinned by resilient domestic
demand, robust investment activity and improving industrial performance.
Industrial output and exports have remained healthy despite external headwinds, with engineering
exports demonstrating resilience, while the automotive sector continues to exhibit robust momentum.
Strong public and private sector investments across infrastructure, manufacturing and emerging
industries continue to support the capex cycle and long-term growth prospects.
However, elevated energy prices and inflationary pressures arising from a deficient monsoon remain key
risks, prompting the RBI to adopt a more hawkish stance and raise pol icy rates. Despite these challenges,
India's economic fundamentals remain supportive of sustained growth. Reflecting confidence in the
economy's underlying strength, the RBI has raised its FY2027 GDP growth forecast to 7.1% from 6.7%
earlier.
