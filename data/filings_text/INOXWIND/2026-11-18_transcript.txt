Balance sheet and working capital


An important development is that our debtor levels have remained broadly stable compared with
March 2026, despite revenue growth during H1, meaning that collections have been at par with
sales. Further, our working capital days appear elevated on a half-year basis due to two reasons.
First, our business is seasonally weighted towards H2, which typically contributes
approximately 70% of annual revenue.


Hence, on a full-year basis, working capital days would be much lower with similar receivables,
as incremental sales as well as the previous receivables are being collected and the full-year
revenue is much higher than H1 revenue.


Consequently, H1 working capital does not fully reflect the annualized position. Second,
inventory increased during Q2 as we prepared for higher deliveries in H2. We expect inventory
levels to moderate by March 2027 as these deliveries are executed.


Overall, we remain focused on accelerating collections, improving working capital efficiency,
and maintaining discipline on the quality of business undertaken.


Order inflows and order book


Inox Wind received approximately 350 MW of orders during Q2, taking H1 FY27 order inflows
to approximately 1.85 GW.


During the quarter, we received repeat orders from NLC India Limited, Terra Clean Energy, a
wholly-owned subsidiary of Indian Oil Corporation, and an IPP. Our order book stood at
approximately 3.8 GW as of October 2026, providing execution visibility of over 24 months.


The reduction in the order book is attributable to the discontinuation of certain orders as part of
our strategic realignment. We believe the quality and executability of order book are more
important than its absolute size. Our customer relationships remain strong across PSUs, IPPs,
C&I, captive, retail, and Inox Clean Energy.


The pipeline remains robust with approximately 5 gigawatt of potential tenders from NTPC,
Indian Oil Corporation, and SECI over the next four to five months, apart from ongoing
negotiations. We also expect recurring opportunities from Inox Clean Energy over the coming
years. As of October 26, the share of equipment supply in our third-party order book stood at
approximately 61%, with the balance 39% being turnkey.


Our medium-term objective remains to increase equipment supply share to approximately 75%
of the third-party order book, thereby improving earnings quality, reducing working capital
intensity, and strengthening cash generation.

Product development and manufacturing expansion


Progress on our next-generation 4.X wind turbine platform is going well. The prototype has been
installed, commissioning is expected shortly, and commercial launch is targeted by the end of
financial year 2027. Our Rohika facility in Gujarat is fully geared to produce blades catering to
the 4.X platform.


Our expansion plans at Inox Renewable Solutions Limited are also progressing well. We have
enhanced operational transformer manufacturing capacity to cater to the 4.X series and plan to
expand into medium-sized transformers in the 8-20 MVA range and larger transformers.


Beyond transformers, we are also progressing with plans to manufacture high-value-added
products such as inverters, unit substations, and electronic control systems. These initiatives will
allow us to participate in a wider part of renewable energy value chain, improve value addition,
and create incremental revenue and margin opportunities.


These investments are being undertaken in a calibrated manner with focus on relatively short
payback periods and attractive returns. Separately, pursuant to the demerger of the power
evacuation infrastructure business from Inox Green into IRSL, the latter is progressing towards
its independent listing. The information memorandum has been filed with stock exchanges, with
listing subject to regulatory approvals.


With respect to Inox Green, the company has successfully raised INR300 crores through a QIP
in October. The proceeds were primarily utilized to complete the payment towards transfer of
Wind World's O&M business to Inox Green. More details on this would be shared by Mathu.


Industry outlook and macroeconomic environment


The macroeconomic environment for the Indian renewable energy sector remains extremely
encouraging, with wind energy playing an increasingly important role in India's energy
transition. 1.8 gigawatt of wind capacity was commissioned in India in Q2 FY27.


This takes the total wind capacity installed in H1 FY27 to 3.2 gigawatt compared to 6.05
gigawatt installed in entire FY26. As mentioned earlier, the wind industry is H2 heavy. As per
the MNRE, approximately 7.5 gigawatt to 8 gigawatt of wind capacity addition is expected to
take place in India in FY27.


We believe India is entering a structurally stronger growth phase for wind energy, with annual
additions potentially reaching 8 gigawatt to 10 gigawatt for the near to medium term. The nature
of renewable energy procurement is evolving favorably for wind, with increasing emphasis on
round-the-clock renewable energy, firm and dispatchable renewable energy and hybrid projects.
Wind is becoming an increasingly important component of the overall energy mix.
